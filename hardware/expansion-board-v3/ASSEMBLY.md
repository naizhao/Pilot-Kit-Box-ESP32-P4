## Before you start

**Run the bare-board checks first: power-to-ground insulation + inner-layer continuity.** Once parts are placed, these can no longer be measured—decoupling capacitors and chips end up in parallel between
power and ground, so the readings are no longer open-circuit. (The full board-acceptance procedure lives in the internal engineering records.)

### Sequencing principle: large before small, center before periphery

Whether you reflow the whole board or blow each part with hot air individually, the conclusion is the same:

- **Whole-board reflow**—large parts need repeated alignment adjustments; if 0402s are already placed around them, tweezers will knock them askew

- **Part-by-part hot air**—when blowing the periphery, the hot air flips over small parts that are already placed; large parts have high thermal mass and, the other way around, are not afraid of it

So within each phase below, parts are **sorted by footprint area from large to small**—just place them top to bottom following the table.

### A few easy ways to get burned

- **Moisture-sensitive plastic-encapsulated parts like BNO085 / BMP388 / CC1312R**. If they have been out of the sealed bag for more than a week, bake them at 125°C for 4 hours before placement; otherwise internal moisture vaporizes during reflow and cracks the package open (popcorn effect)—completely invisible from the outside, and the symptom is the chip simply not working
- **Parts with bottom pads (★★★) are out of a soldering iron's reach**; if hot air blows them askew you can only remove and redo them, so confirm alignment repeatedly before applying heat—don't count on fixing it afterwards
- **Always turn the airflow down for 0402s**; at nominal airflow, blowing an 0402 launches the part right off the pad, and the escaped part often lands on another pad and causes a short
- **pin1 orientation follows the board silkscreen** (notch/dot); the "rotation" column below is only a reference. Component orientation on this board had errors early on—there was a time when 25 two-pin parts had pin1/pin2 reversed
